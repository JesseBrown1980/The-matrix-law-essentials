## Test arms and result

The calibrated **intramolecular** Morse potential, CO parameters, Planck coordinate conventions, and RK4 reference are exactly those in [the first CO molecular slice test](CO-MOLECULAR-SLICES-20261106.md). For the ensemble test the molecules are independent in arms 1–2 and coupled by a **specified toy spring** in arm 3. That spring has **not** been calibrated to real CO–CO intermolecular interactions.

1. **Coherent identical Morse molecules (1–729), time ~185.118 fs.** Every molecule starts with the same initial displacement and evolves under the same deterministic force law. Replicating the measured one-molecule trajectory across N molecules leaves the mean error and the per-molecule RMS exactly the same: `2.917008941648e-5 Å` for each N. Adding copies cannot cancel common numerical bias. This arm shares a trajectory (one simulated trajectory copied N times), unlike arm 3.

2. **Independently phased harmonic CO stretch modes, time ~1.21456 ns.** Molecule `j` starts with phase `2πj/N`, and the true harmonic reference is a continuous cosine. Exact discrete matrix powers are computed for the velocity-Verlet step, not for the nonlinear Morse force. At `N=3,9,27,81,243,729` the *mean signed error* is approximately zero (within about `4e-63 Å` at the chosen 65-digit Decimal precision). But individual-molecule RMS is the same positive `0.01313502968895 Å` for **every** N. At `N=1` the single coherent phase has error `0.01696656816327 Å`. This cancellation is guaranteed by balanced phases; it does not require hundreds of molecules.

3. **Actually updated N-body nearest-neighbor harmonic array (3–729), time ~185.118 fs.** Evolve all N molecules with `x_i''=−ω²x_i−(ω²/9)(2x_i−x_(i−1)−x_(i+1))`, periodic indices. The initial pattern repeats `(A,−A/2,−A/2)`, so its wavenumber is fixed at `2π/3`. Its collective frequency is `ω sqrt(4/3)` for every N multiple of 3. The exact Fourier-mode matrix prediction and direct N-body update agreed to within `2.1e-64 Å` at 65-digit precision. The **RMS individual deviation from the analytical continuum coupled harmonic model remains `0.0001153016861026 Å`** for all N. The largest local deviation is `0.0001630612082508 Å`. This is a genuine N-body interaction simulation *of the stated toy spring law*, not an independently validated physical CO–CO force.

All results are from the **same fixed fine time step** and from an explicitly chosen family of symmetric initial states. The ensemble is larger, but the lattice time resolution is not finer.

## Why the average becomes zero but the discrepancy survives

Write the harmonic discrete propagator after `n` fine time steps as `M_h^n` and the exact continuous harmonic rotation as `R(ω n h)`. Let `D=M_h^n−R`. For phases `θ_j=2πj/N`, define initial state `(A cos θ_j,−A sin θ_j)`. Position error is

`e_j=A(D_11 cos θ_j−D_12 sin θ_j)`.

For all `N≥3`, discrete Fourier orthogonality gives

`mean(e_j)=0`, while
`RMS(e_j)²=A² (D_11²+D_12²)/2`.

Thus the ensemble mean can be **exactly zero** in real arithmetic while RMS stays strictly positive. More molecules alone cannot make a fixed nonzero propagator error vanish. In finite Decimal arithmetic the mean is about 3e-63 Å, not literally zero.

The same idea survives when the toy molecular coordinates are coupled: on a periodically repeated collective mode, `N` changes multiplicity but not the mode's frequency, its discrete time-step bias, or its root-mean-square error.

## What this does and does not answer

This falsifies the universal hypothesis that simply increasing the number of molecules will make the discretization error disappear **for these model classes and states**. It does **not** claim that all many-molecule models have nonvanishing macroscopic bias. Statistical cancellation of noisy **mean observables**, thermalization, averaging, and collective dynamics can change *which* macroscopic quantities approximate continuum physics.

To study actual molecular interactions next, use a calibrated inter-molecular potential or electronic structure method, specify temperature/density/boundary conditions, test correlation functions and spectra (not only mean positions), and refine both spatial and temporal resolution. Time-step bias must be controlled separately from finite-size and noise error. This experiment does **not** test physical Planck discreteness, real quantum vacuum behavior, or the 4D SU(2) continuum Yang–Mills mass gap.
